Fused frontend across GPU architectures
=======================================

The fused Triton kernel measured against the reference on six GPUs spanning four
tensor-core generations. Speedup is reference / fused p50 at the given cell, with
kernels pre-warmed so autotuning does not pollute the first measurement.
Reproduce per GPU with: PYTHONPATH=src python bench/arch_report.py

GPU            gen        cap   fp16 1/1s  fp16 8/10s  fp16 32/30s   fp32 range
RTX 4090       Ada        8.9     1.24x      1.18x        1.06x      0.92 to 1.04x
L4             Ada        8.9     1.34x      1.13x        1.12x      0.92 to 1.04x
A40            Ampere     8.6     1.47x      1.13x        1.21x      0.83 to 1.05x
A100 80GB      Ampere     8.0     1.09x      1.07x        1.25x      0.93 to 0.99x
H100           Hopper     9.0     0.91x      1.06x        1.13x      0.80 to 0.93x
B200           Blackwell  10.0    0.97x      0.92x        1.11x      0.78 to 0.87x

Absolute fused fp16 latency at batch 32, 30 s (hardware speed ranking):
  B200 0.64 ms, H100 0.86 ms, A100 1.43 ms, RTX 4090 1.78 ms, A40 3.10 ms, L4 7.04 ms

Findings
--------
The fusion win is largest on bandwidth-limited cards and smallest on the newest
flagships. The A40 and L4, which use GDDR memory and modest compute, show the
biggest fp16 gains (up to 1.47x on short clips, and still 1.12 to 1.21x at large
batch). The H100 and B200, whose cuBLAS and tensor cores are extremely strong,
show little or no gain on small inputs and can lose, because the vendor baseline
is already near optimal and our kernel's overhead is a larger relative share.

fp16 at large batch wins on every GPU (1.06 to 1.25x), because collapsing the
post-FFT stages into one launch helps even when the matmul is compute bound.

fp32 is parity to a small loss everywhere, and the loss grows on the newest cards
(B200 down to 0.78x), where cuBLAS is hardest to beat and fp32 is not where the
fusion helps.

The takeaway: a hand-written fusion adds the most value where the vendor's
baseline is least optimized, which here means older and smaller GPUs, and the
least on the newest high-end hardware. Any single-GPU speedup number is therefore
incomplete without saying which GPU it came from.
